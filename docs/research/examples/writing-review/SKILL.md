# Writing review

Use for concept papers, white papers, essays and research reports when the owner asks for review or rewriting. The owner-supplied material is the starting point. Use outside research only when requested or required to verify a material claim; distinguish it from the original author's position.

Read `references/personas.md` for the eight perspectives. Read `references/reuse-and-quality.md` before substantive design recommendations. Use `references/record-format.md` for an inspectable review record. These three resources can be reused within a review; do not reload a skill for each persona or each round.

## Start
Establish audience, purpose, author thesis, prohibited changes, publication scope, source bundle, actual execution mode and budget. Read the current manuscript; never review only a prior summary.

## Loop
Review the next material weakness, revise coherently, verify the affected claims and preserve an actual revision snapshot and concise findings. Use the explicitly requested round count; otherwise prefer a bounded evidence-driven review. A no-change round is legitimate. Never manufacture a defect, simulated agent response or approval to fill a quota.

## Practical reuse
Check existing internal concepts and source seams, public standards and documented implementations. Explain what is adopted, adapted or deferred and why. Novelty is not a goal. Do not introduce a second loader, planner, store or policy engine merely to host a new role label.

## Final gate
The final editor and auditor review the whole same-version bundle: manuscript, source/adoption record and review criteria. Both must approve their criteria. If a material defect remains, continue in-scope revision within the available budget or report the exact blocker. Do not call sequential personas independent agents. Preserve prior approvals as historical records, not current approvals after material changes.
